2.2 The facts that shape everything downstream

  • GeoLife contains no danger, injury or incident labels. Its only labels are transportation modes (69 users, 14,718 segments). A supervised risk model is therefore impossible, and the trajectory model is unsupervised — its output is a movement anomaly score, never a probability of danger.
  • UCI HAR contains no falls at all — no fall class, no impact annotation. The impact detector's positive class was therefore manufactured by injecting a fall-like transient into real held-out recordings. Every metric for that model measures detection of the injected signature.
  • UniMiB SHAR is the only dataset here with a real recorded outcome (a real fall). That is why the future-risk model is the only one that predicts a future event rather than describing the present.
  • The weather provider actually used is Open-Meteo, not OpenWeatherMap. The brief named OpenWeatherMap, which needs a key we did not have; the fallback is stated on every artefact.
  • Two corrections are recorded rather than hidden: 698,900 duplicate (trajectory, timestamp) rows were removed from GeoLife, and one corrupt UniMiB recording (subject 10, Jumping, trial 1 — 9,369 non-finite values of 9,624) was dropped and counted.

3.2 UCI HAR

  • Shape: 561 features per window; 7,352 train / 2,947 test windows; 6 activities; raw window 128 samples @ 50 Hz (2.56 s), 9 inertial channels.
  • Split: 21 train / 9 test subjects, overlap 0 — genuinely subject-disjoint.
  • Class balance: near-uniform (train 986–1,407; test 420–537 per activity). Accuracy is therefore a legitimate headline; no resampling or class weighting was needed.
  • Gravity sanity check: mean total-acceleration magnitude 1.0323 g (per-activity 1.0075–1.0516 g) — a real measurement centring on gravity. This is what justifies converting live m/s² samples to g.
  • Signal bands: the three walking variants overlap heavily; sitting/standing/lying separate cleanly. This predicted the eventual confusion pattern exactly.
  • Feature redundancy: the 561 features are heavily correlated; accuracy flattens past ~50 features.

Why the horizon is one second — measured, not chosen: fall trials run 151–446 samples (median 174 = 3.5 s) with the impact near index ~120, leaving ≥ 95 samples of pre-impact signal. 4. The models and what they measured

4.1 Trajectory anomaly detector (GeoLife, unsupervised)

IsolationForest, 200 trees, contamination 0.02, seed 42, over 14 standardised movement features. Score = -score_samples(scaled_features); higher = more anomalous, not a probability. Trained on 1,060,493 points (900 trajectories), evaluated on 369,247 points (300 trajectories), split by traj_uid with 0 overlap.

Accuracy, F1 and a confusion matrix do not exist for this model, and can't: GeoLife has no anomaly labels, so there is nothing to score against. Any such figure would be invented.

So it was validated by injection on real data: GPS teleport bursts (0.02°, run length 3) planted into real held-out trajectories.

How to read this. The shuffled-label control collapses to chance, so the near-perfect score is the detector genuinely finding the injected signature in real held-out subjects' recordings — not leakage. But the positives were manufactured, so this measures detection of a planted fall, not of a real person falling. §7.2 states the consequence.

One injected impact. A large, stereotyped, high-signal transient produced by a single generator — which is exactly why 0.9999 cannot be read as fall-detection performance.

6. Four risk concepts, kept separate

Shown as What it actually is Trained? Has a probability?
Geofence status A configured rule: inside/outside a polygon the authority drew No No
Approach risk A published weighted checklist over crossing history No — documented heuristic No
Activity A 6-class classifier over the current 2.56 s window Yes Confidence only
Movement / future risk An impact detector and a 1-second-ahead impact forecast Yes Yes, calibrated (future-risk)

Enforced in code and tests: distinct types throughout, so a screen cannot render the heuristic under the trained model's heading; the card's own text names the other concepts and denies being them (asserted by a test); risk never triggers SOS — backend/tsm/api.py ignores risk entirely when computing state, and a HIGH prediction leaves latestSos empty and openSosCount == 0 (asserted by backend/tests/test_risk.py). Predictions live in their own table, their own endpoints, and their own dashboard panel — never the SOS queue or the incident feed.

7.2 Limits of this evidence

These are properties of what was built, so they belong in the report rather than being cut away.

  1. Because the impact detector's positives are synthetic, its 0.9999 is not a fall-detection claim. It measures detection of an injected signature.
  2. The future-risk model raises about 5.09 false alarms per minute of ordinary activity, worst on jumping (16 %), sitting down (16 %) and lying down (13 %), and its mid-range probabilities are over-confident. It is an informational band plus a queued summary — never an alarm, and it never sends an SOS.
  3. The label is a fall impact, not a tourist incident. UniMiB is 30 healthy adults in a laboratory; UCI HAR is a scripted waist-mounted protocol. No real incident, injury, crime or medical outcome exists in any dataset used, so no supervised tourist-risk model is possible from this data.
  4. The model cannot tell a dropped phone from a falling person — no phone-drop class exists in any dataset used.
  5. Nothing here has run on a physical phone or emulator, and the app's screens, sensors, GPS and HTTP stack have therefore not been exercised on hardware.
  6. No user study, interview, survey or usability evaluation was conducted at any point.
  7. Some metrics do not exist and are not estimated: real-anomaly accuracy/F1 for the unsupervised model (no labels); ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, per-class specificity and calibration for the HAR and transport models (never saved); repeated cross-validated subject-disjoint evaluation (single fold only); the impact detector's training-window count.
  8. Public and laboratory datasets do not validate real tourist safety. Every metric here is a statement about models on held-out laboratory and public data — not about a tourist.
